About this tool

Finds photos in a Wikimedia Commons category that are not yet used in any Wikipedia article and depict a person, then pairs each with that person's current Wikipedia infobox photo so you can see whether the new photo is an improvement.

Searching

  • Category — a Commons category, with or without the Category: prefix. Spaces, underscores and + all work.
  • Start / End date — filters by the file's upload date. Previous month, This month and This year prefill common ranges.

Toggles

  • Hide same-author candidates (on) — hides a candidate when the current infobox photo is by the same photographer and is already good, so another of their photos adds nothing.
  • Hide when current photo is good (on) — hides a match when the infobox photo is already good, whoever took it.
  • Show no-depicts photos (off) — switches to showing only unused photos with no depicts statement. Nothing to compare, so these are a "needs a depicts statement added" list, with search links that guess the person's name from the filename.
  • Show category photos already in use (off) — switches to showing only category photos that are an article's current infobox photo, each with a button to fetch the previous photo by a different author.

The last two are exclusive views: turning one on replaces the comparison results and disables the other toggles.

What "good" means

A photo counts as good when it is present, not stale (taken within the last 12 months, falling back to its upload date) and not low-res (2 megapixels or more). Stale dates and low resolutions are marked in red.

Buttons on each row

  • Copy — copies the bare filename (no File: prefix) ready to paste into an infobox |image= field.
  • Edit — opens the article in the wikitext editor.
  • Find previous photo by a different author — walks the article's revision history and shows the previous infobox photo that someone else took, with when and by whom it was replaced.

Notes

  • The "current infobox photo" is the image the article actually displays, which often differs from the photo set on Wikidata.
  • A photo can be live on several language Wikipedias; each appears as its own row, because each wiki has its own history.
  • All data comes from read-only calls to the public Wikimedia APIs. If a search fails with a "busy" message, Wikidata is lagging — wait and retry.
